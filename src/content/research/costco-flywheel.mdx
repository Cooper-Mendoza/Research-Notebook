## The membership fee as signal, not revenue

Most people think Costco is a retailer with a membership fee. That framing is backwards.

Costco is a membership business that happens to sell goods. The distinction matters because it changes what they're optimizing for. A retailer maximizes margin per SKU. A membership business maximizes renewal rate — which means it has to justify the fee every year. The mechanism for justifying the fee is the deal. If the deals aren't good enough, you don't renew.

This creates a rare internal discipline: Costco's finance team is structurally forced to compete against itself every twelve months. That's unusual in retail.

## The SKU constraint as competitive weapon

Costco carries roughly 4,000 SKUs. A typical Walmart Supercenter carries around 142,000. That compression is not a limitation — it's the source of their pricing power.

When you buy 4,000 SKUs in Costco volumes, you are the only customer that matters to many of your suppliers. That leverage flows downstream to members as price. You can't replicate this with 30,000 SKUs because the volume per SKU drops to the point where you're just another channel.

| Metric | Costco | Walmart | Target |
|--------|--------|---------|--------|
| SKUs | ~4,000 | ~142,000 | ~80,000 |
| Revenue/sqft | ~$1,200 | ~$450 | ~$340 |
| Gross Margin | ~13% | ~24% | ~30% |
| Operating Income Source | Membership | Merchandise | Merchandise |

The low gross margin is the point. Costco's stated policy is to cap markup at 14% on branded goods and 15% on Kirkland. Every dollar of operating income above that notional return goes back to members in the form of lower prices. This is not marketing copy — it's enforced by the renewal rate. If they broke the deal, you'd know it.

## What I got wrong the first time I looked at this

I initially dismissed Costco as a low-growth compounder with thin margins and a mature US footprint. That view missed two things.

First, international is earlier-stage than the US business and structurally identical. The flywheel works the same way in South Korea as it does in Arizona. It just has fewer warehouses.

Second, the e-commerce fear is misapplied. Costco sells pallets of paper towels and 10-pound bags of almonds. The economics of shipping that last-mile are terrible for Amazon and fine for Costco, because Costco members drive to the warehouse to pick it up. The warehouse visit is the product. Treasure hunt is not a metaphor — it's a designed experience that drives add-on purchases. You cannot replicate it online.
